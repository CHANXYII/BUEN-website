Member panel — five directions

Same five Gen 2 ambassadors, same photos, same palette. What changes is how a visitor meets them. Each one below is live: hover, drag, click, tab through it.

Direction A

Roster index

No photo grid at all. The panel is a magazine contributors page: five names set large on hairlines, major on the right. The portrait only exists while you are reading a name — it wipes up on a plate that follows your cursor, and the four names you are not reading recede. Scales to Gen 3, 4, 5 without touching the layout.

Move your pointer down the names · tab through them

    Direction B

    Slat accordion

    Five tall slats fill the width like a folded screen; names run vertically up the edge. Touch one and it pushes the others aside, the photo comes up out of grayscale into full colour, and the detail block fades in. All five faces stay on screen the whole time — nothing is ever hidden behind a click.

    Hover a slat · click to hold it open · arrow keys

    Direction C

    Contact sheet

    A strip of 35 mm film, sprocket edge and frame numbers included — the ambassadors' actual medium is the camera. The strip scrubs under your hand; whichever frame lands in the centre develops: grayscale lifts to colour, the frame widens, the caption comes up to full strength. Everything is driven by scroll position, so the motion is yours, not a loop.

    Drag the strip sideways · scroll it · it snaps

    Direction D

    Lanyard wall

    Ambassadors work in badges, so the panel is the badge rail. Five ID cards hang from one hairline on drawn cords, each breathing on its own slow phase so the wall is never static. Reach for one and it swings on its clip; click and it turns over — the back of the card carries the positions and the year, where a real badge carries them.

    Hover to swing · click a badge to turn it over

    Direction E

    Letterform marquee

    Built from what the page already does: the outlined word that scrolls behind the president banner, now carrying the roster. Five surnames drift past as hollow letterforms; put your pointer on one and the whole line stops dead and that person's photograph fills the inside of their own name. The reader line underneath picks up who you are holding.

    Hover a surname to stop the line and fill it
